Ashwagandha extract pricing from India is not a single number. It is a matrix of variables -- withanolide specification, source material, organic certification, order volume, trading structure, and the time of year you are buying. Buyers who approach the market without understanding these variables frequently pay more than necessary, or receive quotes that cannot be meaningfully compared against each other.

This guide is a transparent pricing reference for international buyers. It explains the variables that drive ashwagandha extract pricing from India, provides indicative price ranges for the most common specifications, and explains the gap between the FOB India number on a supplier's quotation and the actual landed cost in your market.

Pricing data reflects market conditions in late 2026 and is indicative rather than contractual -- raw material markets move seasonally, and individual supplier pricing varies by relationship and volume commitment.

These are manufacturer-direct price ranges. Trading company pricing for equivalent specifications typically sits 15 to 30 percent higher.

## Variable 1: Withanolide Specification

The withanolide percentage on the Certificate of Analysis is the primary commercial specification for ashwagandha extract and the primary driver of price. The relationship is not linear -- achieving higher withanolide concentrations requires more raw material input per kg of finished extract, and at higher percentages, the extraction process becomes more complex and yield falls.

**2.5% withanolides** is the entry specification, used in value supplement formulations and markets where price sensitivity dominates. Indian manufacturers can produce this specification at comparatively low cost because the extraction requirement is modest.

**5% withanolides** is the market standard. The largest share of global ashwagandha extract volume is sold at this specification. Price competition is highest here because the specification is available from a large number of Indian producers.

**7% withanolides** is a premium specification. The raw material requirement per kg of finished extract is approximately 40 percent higher than at 5%, and the extraction process more demanding. Not all manufacturers can consistently achieve 7% from root-only material -- some blend in leaf-derived extract, which is less expensive to produce but changes the phytochemical profile.

**10% withanolides and above** is a specialist specification, typically used in concentrated capsule or powder-sachet formats. Only a subset of Indian manufacturers can reliably produce and verify this specification from root-only material under GMP conditions, which limits supply options and supports a higher price.

## Variable 2: Source Material -- Root vs Root-and-Leaf Blend

The second most significant price driver, and the one most frequently obscured in supplier quotations, is whether the extract is derived exclusively from root material or from a blend of root and leaf.

Ashwagandha leaf contains higher concentrations of certain withanolides than root. This means leaf-derived extract can hit a target withanolide percentage at lower raw material cost than root-only extract. A supplier producing 5% withanolides from a 70:30 root-to-leaf blend can undercut a root-only producer on price -- sometimes by 20 to 35 percent for the same stated specification.

For most international buyers, root-only extract is the requirement. The clinical evidence base and consumer expectation for ashwagandha is built on root preparations. If a quotation for 5% withanolides looks significantly below the market range above, ask explicitly whether the extract is root-only or a root-and-leaf blend. A credible supplier will confirm this in writing and the Certificate of Analysis will state the plant part used.

For more on root vs root-and-leaf tradeoffs, see our [ashwagandha extract buyer's guide](/blog/ashwagandha-extract-buyers-guide/).

## Variable 3: Organic Certification

Organic certification adds cost at two levels: the raw material level (organic ashwagandha root costs more than conventional, and the certified organic supply base is more constrained) and the certification overhead level (maintaining organic certification requires annual audits, transaction certificates per lot, and documentation that non-certified facilities do not carry).

Indicative organic premiums over equivalent non-organic specification:

- **NPOP (India national organic program):** 40 to 60 percent premium over non-organic
- **USDA NOP:** 70 to 90 percent premium (requires NOP-accredited certification body audit, which costs more than NPOP-only)
- **EU Organic (Regulation 2018/848):** 65 to 85 percent premium

These premiums compress at high volumes (1 MT and above) because certified organic producers compete for long-term volume commitments. Buyers who can commit to annual volumes should use that commitment as leverage in organic price negotiations.

A critical compliance note: an organic certificate from an NPOP-accredited body does not automatically satisfy NOP or EU organic requirements. If your formulation carries an organic claim in a regulated market, confirm that the certification body is accredited under the standard applicable in your destination market -- not just the Indian domestic standard.

## Variable 4: Order Volume and Pricing Tiers

Indian ashwagandha extract manufacturers price by volume tier, with meaningful step-downs at key thresholds. Indicative tiers for standard 5% withanolides non-organic root:

| Volume | Indicative FOB Price Range |
|---|---|
| Sample (100-500 g) | USD 40-80/kg (sample rate, not commercial) |
| Trial order (10-25 kg) | USD 28-40/kg |
| Small commercial (100-200 kg) | USD 22-30/kg |
| Mid-volume (500 kg-1 MT) | USD 18-26/kg |
| Volume commitment (2 MT+) | USD 14-22/kg |
| Annual contract (5 MT+) | USD 12-18/kg |

The gap between sample pricing and volume contract pricing is substantial. Buyers who have only seen sample-size quotations should not assume those reflect commercial pricing. Conversely, buyers who quote their annual volume requirement upfront -- even if placing a smaller first order -- typically receive better pricing than buyers who negotiate each order in isolation.

## Variable 5: Trading Structure -- Manufacturer Direct vs Trading Company

A supplier's position in the supply chain adds cost directly and, from the buyer's perspective, invisibly.

An Indian herbal ingredient trading company buys finished extract from manufacturers and resells it to international buyers. Their margin sits between the manufacturer's export price and your purchase price. This margin is not stated on their quotation -- their invoice simply reflects the price they choose to charge. Typical trading company margins on ashwagandha extract: 15 to 35 percent above the manufacturer's direct export price for equivalent specification and certification.

For buyers with sufficient volume to be commercially interesting to manufacturers directly -- generally 100 kg and above per order -- this margin is accessible. Accessing manufacturer-direct pricing through a commission-based sourcing partner (who earns their fee from the supplier side, not added to your purchase price) captures this margin without requiring the buyer to independently manage manufacturer relationships and qualification.

The practical implication: if you receive a quote for 5% withanolides non-organic at USD 38/kg for a 200 kg order, you may be looking at trading company pricing on a specification that a manufacturer-direct buyer accesses at USD 24-28/kg. For how to determine whether you are dealing with a manufacturer or a trader, see our [manufacturer vs trader identification guide](/blog/manufacturer-vs-trader-india-herbal-ingredient/).

## Variable 6: Seasonal Price Movement

Ashwagandha is a Rabi (winter) crop in India, harvested primarily between January and March. The post-harvest period (March to June) typically offers the best pricing conditions as fresh-crop raw material is abundant. Price tends to firm in the second half of the year (August to December) as raw material stocks are drawn down ahead of the next harvest.

Buyers with predictable annual requirements should plan to negotiate and lock pricing -- or at least raw material forward commitments -- in the March to May window, when Indian manufacturers are most motivated to convert harvest inventory into contracted orders.

Seasonal price movement for ashwagandha extract at the same specification can range from 10 to 20 percent between the most and least favourable timing in a normal year. Poor harvests, drought years, or sudden demand spikes can produce larger swings.

## Variable 7: Third-Party Testing Scope

The scope of independent laboratory testing included with each batch affects the unit price. A supplier who conducts HPLC withanolide assay, HPTLC botanical identity confirmation, heavy metals by ICP-MS (arsenic, cadmium, lead, mercury), full pesticide residue screening, and microbial panel -- all from an NABL-accredited or internationally recognised laboratory -- carries more per-batch cost than one who runs only in-house testing.

For buyers supplying regulated markets (EU, USA, Japan), this additional cost is not optional -- the testing is a regulatory requirement. Buyers are better served by a price that includes comprehensive third-party testing than by a lower price that shifts testing cost to them later, or that results in an import refusal because the documentation is not accepted by the destination authority.

## FOB India vs. Landed Cost

The FOB India price on a supplier's quotation represents the cost to the point of loading at an Indian port. It does not represent your landed cost. To convert, add:

**Ocean freight.** LCL (less than container load) shipments of 100 to 500 kg from India to Europe or North America typically cost USD 150 to 350 per 100 kg, including origin charges, depending on route, carrier, and season. Air freight for smaller or urgent shipments is substantially higher: USD 5 to 12 per kg.

**Import duty.** Ashwagandha extract classified under HS 1302.19 carries varying duty rates by destination. The USA currently applies a zero percent MFN rate on this HS heading for most origins. The EU applies 3.2 percent on the standard tariff line. Australia charges zero under general tariff. The GCC rate varies by member state. Where India has an operative trade agreement with the destination (the UAE-India CEPA is in force; EU-India FTA negotiations continue), preferential rates may apply -- confirm current eligibility with your customs broker for each shipment.

**Freight insurance.** Typically 0.2 to 0.5 percent of the CIF cargo value.

**Destination customs clearance.** Customs broker charges and import handling fees: typically USD 150 to 400 per shipment for standard botanical extract imports, varying by destination.

**Worked example:** A 200 kg shipment of 5% withanolides non-organic at USD 24/kg FOB India, shipping to the EU:

- Product: USD 4,800
- Ocean freight LCL (estimate): USD 480
- Import duty (3.2% of CIF): approximately USD 175
- Insurance: USD 25
- Customs clearance: USD 200
- **Estimated landed cost: USD 5,680, or approximately USD 28.40/kg**

The FOB price of USD 24/kg becomes a landed price of approximately USD 28.40/kg -- an 18 percent uplift before the buyer has sold a single unit.

## Red Flags in Pricing

**Prices significantly below the market range.** If a supplier quotes 5% withanolides root-only at USD 10/kg FOB India for a 200 kg order, one of three outcomes is probable: the specification is not what is stated (root-and-leaf blend, low actual withanolide content, or adulterated material); the certification claimed is not valid; or the quotation is a hook intended to open a conversation, with the real price appearing later. None of these outcomes serves the buyer.

**Sample pricing presented as commercial pricing.** Suppliers occasionally quote attractive small-batch pricing to close a sample inquiry, then revise substantially upward for the commercial order. Confirm explicitly whether the quoted price applies at your target commercial volume.

**All-in prices with no specification breakdown.** A credible manufacturer can give you the component price for product, a freight estimate, and certification costs separately. A supplier who quotes a single delivered number with no breakdown is either a trader passing through a cost structure they do not fully understand, or is obscuring a margin that would be visible if disaggregated.

## What Buyers in Different Regions Actually Pay

**EU buyers** typically require EU organic certification and comprehensive pesticide residue testing compliant with EU MRLs. These requirements mean EU buyers rarely access the bottom of the non-organic price range even when buying conventional specification. Effective landed cost for EU-compliant organic ashwagandha is commonly in the range of EUR 38 to 55 per kg.

**USA buyers** sourcing for DSHEA-compliant dietary supplement formulations typically require cGMP-facility sourcing and HPLC CoA per USP-NF method. The US market carries moderate price sensitivity for well-documented specification. FOB India pricing of USD 20 to 26 per kg for 5% withanolides non-organic with appropriate documentation is common for mid-volume buyers.

**GCC buyers** prioritise Halal certification alongside GMP. Halal-certified ashwagandha extract carries a small premium over non-Halal equivalent specification -- typically 5 to 10 percent -- reflecting the certification overhead. For more on the UAE-India CEPA duty implications for herbal ingredients, see our [CEPA guide for the GCC](/blog/india-uae-cepa-herbal-ingredients-gcc/).

**Japan and South Korea buyers** require quality standards consistent with Japan Health Food Association (JHFA) norms or equivalent, and Japanese- or Korean-language documentation. The compliance overhead pushes effective pricing higher than the base FOB ranges above suggest.

**Australian buyers** sourcing for TGA-listed medicines are subject to PIC/S GMP facility requirements. Buyers sourcing for TGA-listed products face the highest compliance documentation overhead in this market and should expect pricing at or above the upper end of the ranges shown in this guide.

## How to Get an Accurate Quote

The most common reason international buyers receive unhelpful or misleading quotes from Indian ashwagandha extract suppliers is that the inquiry is underspecified. A supplier who receives "Please quote ashwagandha extract 500 kg" will fill the gaps themselves -- often in ways that favour their margin over the buyer's actual requirements.

A well-specified inquiry includes:

- Withanolide specification, percent by HPLC, and whether USP-NF method is required
- Source material: root-only required, or root-and-leaf acceptable
- Organic certification requirement: none, NPOP, NOP, or EU organic
- Heavy metals and pesticide testing scope required
- Halal certification: yes or no
- Destination port and approximate shipping month
- Target commercial volume per order, and indicative annual volume
- Preferred payment terms

Providing this information upfront removes the supplier's ability to quote something cheaper and different from what you need, and allows you to compare quotations on an equivalent basis.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the current price of ashwagandha extract from India?**

As of late 2026, standard 5% withanolides non-organic ashwagandha extract trades in the range of USD 18 to 28 per kg FOB India for commercial quantities of 100 kg and above. Certified organic 5% withanolides runs USD 32 to 48 per kg FOB India. High-potency 7% to 10% withanolides commands USD 40 to 65 per kg FOB depending on specification and certification. These are manufacturer-direct price ranges; trading company pricing typically sits 15 to 30 percent above these levels for equivalent specifications.

**What factors most affect ashwagandha extract price from India?**

The four primary price drivers are withanolide specification, source material (root-only vs root-and-leaf blend), organic certification status, and order volume. Secondary drivers include extraction method, third-party testing scope, the supplier's trading structure, and seasonal raw material availability. The difference between the lowest and highest price for notionally identical 5% withanolides specification can exceed 100 percent when all these variables are considered.

**How much more does organic ashwagandha extract cost compared to conventional?**

Certified organic ashwagandha extract from India typically carries a 60 to 90 percent premium over equivalent non-organic specification, depending on the certification standard and current organic raw material availability. NPOP certification carries the smallest premium. USDA NOP or EU organic-compliant certification carries a larger premium. At commercial volumes of 500 kg and above, the organic-conventional price gap can narrow as certified organic suppliers compete for volume commitments.

**What is the difference between FOB India price and landed cost for ashwagandha extract?**

FOB India price covers the cost of product and export documentation to the point of loading at an Indian port. To convert to landed cost, add: ocean freight (typically USD 150 to 350 per 100 kg for LCL shipments to Europe, North America, or the GCC), import duty in the destination country (0% to 10% depending on country and HS classification), freight insurance, and destination customs clearance costs. For EU, UK, or Japan buyers, total landed cost is typically 20 to 40 percent above the FOB India price.

**How does ashwagandha extract pricing differ between a manufacturer and a trading company in India?**

Indian herbal ingredient trading companies typically add 15 to 35 percent above the manufacturer's direct export price for equivalent specification and certification.
