When an international buyer decides to source herbal ingredients from India for the first time, one of the earliest questions they face is not which ingredient to source or which supplier to use - it is which sourcing channel to use to access Indian suppliers at all.

The options are meaningfully different. Buying directly from an Indian manufacturer, working through a trading company, engaging a specialist sourcing agent on a commission model, and using a B2B marketplace each offer a distinct set of tradeoffs across cost, control, trust, risk, and operational overhead. Choosing the wrong channel for your situation does not just cost money - it can result in quality failures, regulatory non-compliance, relationship breakdown, and wasted months.

This guide maps each of the four main India sourcing channels clearly: what each model is, how it works, what it costs, what it controls, and which buyer profile it suits. It is a decision-framework post, not a sales pitch. All four channels have genuine use cases, and the right answer depends on your volume, experience, internal capability, and risk tolerance.

---

## The Four Channels at a Glance

Before examining each model in detail, it helps to understand the basic structure of each.

**Direct import:** The buyer identifies an Indian manufacturer, qualifies them independently, and contracts directly for supply. No intermediary. The buyer manages the full relationship.

**Trading company:** An intermediary buys Indian herbal ingredients from manufacturers and resells them to international buyers. The trading company takes title to the goods, holds inventory, and manages the supplier relationship on your behalf.

**Sourcing agent (commission model):** A specialist facilitator who identifies, qualifies, and connects buyers to Indian manufacturers - earning a commission on the transaction. The buyer contracts directly with the manufacturer; the agent does not take title to the goods.

**B2B marketplace:** An online platform (IndiaMART, TradeIndia, Alibaba India) where Indian suppliers list their products and international buyers can discover and contact them directly. No intermediary is involved beyond the platform itself.

Each model has a different cost structure, a different control profile, and a different risk profile. The comparison that follows addresses all three dimensions for each channel.

## Model 1: Direct Import from an Indian Manufacturer

### How it works

The buyer sources directly from the Indian manufacturing facility - typically through an export division or dedicated export manager. The buyer qualifies the supplier (site audits, documentation review, sample testing), negotiates pricing and terms, manages purchase orders, reviews export documents, and handles the import process at their end.

### Cost profile

Direct import offers the lowest per-unit cost - no intermediary margin layers exist between you and the manufacturer. For commodity herbal powders (moringa, tulsi, turmeric, ginger), direct import at scale can achieve 15 to 35 percent lower unit costs than equivalent goods sourced through trading companies, depending on the supplier and the order volume. For standardised extracts, the margin difference between direct and intermediated supply narrows but remains material at significant volumes.

### Control profile

Maximum control. The buyer selects the manufacturer, specifies the product to their exact requirement, receives batch-specific documentation directly from source, and has a direct commercial relationship with the producing facility. Any quality issue is addressed with the manufacturer directly, without an intermediary filtering the communication.

### Risk profile

High operational responsibility. The buyer carries the full burden of supplier due diligence - verifying certifications, auditing facilities, reviewing quality systems, and confirming that the supplier can meet the destination market's regulatory requirements. In India's herbal ingredient export sector, where supplier quality varies enormously and certification claims are not always independently verifiable, this due diligence burden is substantial.

### Who this suits

Direct import is the right model for buyers who:

- Have annual requirements of 500 kg or more per ingredient, creating sufficient volume to be commercially attractive to Indian manufacturers without an intermediary's endorsement
- Have in-house quality assurance capability for supplier auditing and sample testing
- Have prior India sourcing experience or are willing to invest the time to develop it
- Are sourcing a small number of well-defined ingredients from stable, known suppliers

For first-time India buyers, small or mid-size brands, and buyers sourcing a diverse range of ingredients from multiple Indian manufacturers, direct import is typically premature - the due diligence overhead is too high, and the risk of a supplier selection error too costly.

## Model 2: Trading Company

### How it works

A trading company is an intermediary that purchases Indian herbal ingredients from manufacturers and resells them to international buyers. The trading company takes ownership of the goods - they hold stock, manage the Indian supplier relationship, handle export documentation, and sell to you under their own invoice. You deal with the trading company, not with the Indian manufacturer.

### Cost profile

Trading companies add a margin at each level of the supply chain: they buy from manufacturers at a lower price and sell to you at a higher one. In practice, trading company margins for Indian herbal ingredients typically range from 15 to 40 percent above the manufacturer's direct export price, depending on the product, the trading company's inventory investment, and their service scope. For high-volume commodity ingredients, this margin represents significant additional cost over the life of a supply relationship.

### Control profile

Low to moderate control. You have visibility of the product you receive, but not of the manufacturing facility, the batch origin, or the documentation trail at source. If a trading company is sourcing from multiple Indian manufacturers and blending stock, traceability is reduced further. If you need a specific Indian manufacturer's product - because of their organic certification, their proprietary extraction process, or a regulatory requirement tied to their specific facility - a trading company is unlikely to offer it.

### Risk profile

Moderate. The trading company absorbs the supplier qualification burden and the inventory risk, which reduces your operational exposure. The risks that remain are documentation opacity (you cannot independently verify the manufacturer's certifications without access to the facility), quality consistency (the trading company may switch manufacturers without informing you), and pricing inflexibility (you have no leverage on the underlying manufacturer price).

### Who this suits

Trading companies suit buyers who:

- Need small or irregular quantities below most Indian manufacturers' direct export MOQs
- Want to minimise their own operational involvement in India supply chain management
- Are sourcing commodity ingredients where traceability and manufacturer-specific certification are not required
- Need a quick supply solution without investing the time in direct supplier qualification

Trading companies are a pragmatic starting point for buyers new to India sourcing who need product quickly and in modest volume. They are a less effective long-term model once volume grows, because the margin cost compounds significantly at scale, and the lack of traceability becomes a compliance risk in markets with stringent documentation requirements (EU, USA, Japan).

## Model 3: Sourcing Agent (Commission Model)

### How it works

A specialist sourcing agent - sometimes called a sourcing partner, India sourcing consultant, or commission agent - facilitates the buyer-manufacturer relationship without taking title to the goods. The agent identifies suitable Indian manufacturers against the buyer's specification, conducts or coordinates supplier qualification, manages the sample process, negotiates pricing on the buyer's behalf, and supports the buyer through the first order and ongoing supply management.

The buyer contracts directly with the Indian manufacturer. The agent earns a commission - typically 3 to 8 percent of the transaction value depending on the service scope - paid by the supplier as a referral or introduction fee. In a well-structured commission model, the buyer's landed cost is not higher than they would achieve through direct import, because the agent's commission is incorporated into the manufacturer's pricing structure rather than added on top.

### Cost profile

Commission models are designed to be cost-neutral for the buyer - the agent's fee is embedded in the manufacturer's pricing, not invoiced separately. In practice, well-connected sourcing agents with strong manufacturer relationships can often achieve pricing for buyers that is comparable to or better than what an inexperienced direct buyer would negotiate independently, because the agent brings volume credibility and repeat business to the manufacturer relationship. The net landed cost difference between a commission-model sourcing agent and a competent direct buyer is typically small.

### Control profile

High. Because the buyer contracts directly with the Indian manufacturer, full traceability is maintained. Batch-specific documentation comes from the manufacturer directly. The buyer has visibility of the supply source, the certifications, and the quality documentation chain - the same visibility they would have in direct import, but with the agent managing the relationship complexity.

### Risk profile

Low to moderate. The agent absorbs the supplier discovery and qualification work, reducing the buyer's due diligence burden. The principal risk is choosing the wrong agent - one who recommends manufacturers based on commission rate rather than quality, or who lacks the India market depth to assess manufacturer capability accurately. This risk is mitigated by selecting an agent with verifiable relationships, transparent processes, and references from existing clients.

### Who this suits

Commission-model sourcing partners are well-suited for:

- First-time India buyers who need the access and knowledge of an experienced intermediary without paying trading company margins
- Small to mid-size brands sourcing 5 to 500 kg quantities per ingredient who are not yet large enough to be commercially compelling to Indian manufacturers without introduction
- Buyers sourcing a diverse range of ingredients from multiple Indian suppliers, where managing direct relationships with each is operationally impractical
- Buyers in markets with complex regulatory requirements (EU novel food, US NDI, Japan health claim registration) where specialist documentation guidance is valuable

## Model 4: B2B Marketplace

### How it works

B2B platforms like IndiaMART, TradeIndia, Global Sources, and Alibaba India aggregate supplier listings from Indian exporters and manufacturers. A buyer searches the platform, contacts listed suppliers directly, and negotiates and purchases independently. The platform provides the discovery infrastructure; the transaction is between buyer and supplier.

### Cost profile

B2B marketplaces offer the theoretical lowest discovery cost - no intermediary fees, direct access to supplier pricing. In practice, marketplace pricing is often not the lowest available, because listed suppliers price for unknown buyers with no relationship leverage, and because a significant proportion of marketplace listings are from traders and aggregators rather than manufacturers, adding their own margin to the underlying manufacturer price.

### Control profile

Variable and unverified. A marketplace listing tells you the supplier's claimed capabilities, certifications, and price - none of which can be assumed accurate without independent verification. The buyer is entirely responsible for distinguishing manufacturers from traders, verifying certification currency and scope, and assessing quality system adequacy. This is non-trivial in India's herbal ingredient sector, where certification claims are widely misrepresented.

### Risk profile

High for first-time buyers. The absence of any intermediary qualification layer means the buyer absorbs all supplier selection risk. The most common outcomes of marketplace-only sourcing without proper due diligence are: receiving product that does not match the specification, receiving product with accurate certifications that nonetheless fails destination-market regulatory requirements, or working with a trader who represents themselves as a manufacturer and cannot provide the facility-level documentation that EU, US, or Japanese import regulations require.

Marketplaces are a useful starting point for market discovery - understanding what suppliers exist, what price ranges look like, what product forms are available. They are not a reliable sourcing channel in themselves without a parallel qualification process that matches the rigour of direct import.

### Who this suits

B2B marketplace discovery is appropriate for:

- Experienced India sourcing teams conducting initial market mapping before direct qualification
- Buyers who have strong in-house supplier qualification processes and can independently verify supplier claims
- Sourcing research and price benchmarking, not as the primary sourcing channel

## How Buyers Typically Progress Through These Models

Most international buyers do not commit to one sourcing model permanently. A common and logical progression looks like this:

**Stage 1 - Discovery:** Use B2B marketplaces to understand what Indian suppliers exist for your ingredients, at what price ranges, and in what product forms. Do not place orders based solely on marketplace contact.

**Stage 2 - First order:** Engage a specialist sourcing agent or place a small first order through a trading company. Prioritise getting a quality-verified product to market quickly over optimising unit cost at this stage.

**Stage 3 - Scale:** As volume grows and you develop familiarity with the Indian supplier landscape, migrate high-volume stable ingredients to direct manufacturer relationships. Retain sourcing partner support for new ingredients, new markets, or complex regulatory situations.

This progression optimises for learning and risk management in the early stages, and for cost efficiency as relationships and knowledge compound.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between a trading company and a sourcing agent when buying herbal ingredients from India?**

A trading company buys herbal ingredients from Indian manufacturers and resells them to international buyers, taking ownership of the goods and carrying inventory risk. A sourcing agent acts as an intermediary without taking title to the goods - the buyer contracts directly with the manufacturer, and the agent earns a commission for facilitating the match, qualification, and transaction. The practical difference is that a trading company controls the supply chain and adds a margin at each level, while a sourcing agent's commercial interest is aligned with helping the buyer find the right manufacturer at the right price.

**When does direct import from an Indian manufacturer make sense for a herbal ingredient buyer?**

Direct import makes commercial sense when a buyer has enough volume to be attractive to Indian manufacturers on their own, has the internal capability to manage supplier qualification, documentation compliance, and shipping logistics without local support, and is sourcing a limited range of ingredients from a stable, well-qualified supplier. Buyers with annual requirements above 500 kg per ingredient, established quality assurance processes, and prior India sourcing experience are the strongest candidates for a direct manufacturer relationship.

**What does a commission-only sourcing model mean and who pays the fee?**

In a commission-only sourcing model, the sourcing agent earns a percentage of the transaction value as a fee for identifying, qualifying, and facilitating the buyer-supplier relationship - without charging the buyer a retainer or upfront consulting fee. The commission is typically paid by the supplier as a referral or introduction fee, meaning the buyer's landed cost is not increased by the agent's involvement. This model aligns the agent's incentive with completing successful transactions rather than generating consulting hours.

**What are the main risks of sourcing herbal ingredients through B2B marketplaces like IndiaMART?**

The primary risk is supplier verification: B2B marketplace listings often include brokers, aggregators, and unverified traders alongside genuine manufacturers, with no independent audit of claimed certifications, capacity, or quality systems. Buyers who select suppliers based on marketplace listings alone frequently encounter quality inconsistency, documentation gaps, or suppliers who cannot meet the regulatory requirements of the destination market. Marketplaces are useful for initial market discovery but are not a substitute for direct supplier qualification, factory auditing, and independent sample testing before committing to a commercial order.

**Which India sourcing model is best for a buyer placing their first order?**

For most buyers placing their first order from India, a specialist sourcing partner operating on a commission model offers the most reliable path. It provides access to pre-qualified manufacturers without requiring the buyer to develop India market knowledge from scratch, reduces the risk of quality or documentation failures on a first order, and avoids the margin layering of a trading company. As the buyer builds direct India supplier relationships over time, they can migrate high-volume stable ingredients to direct import while retaining sourcing partner support for new ingredients or complex regulatory requirements.
