Prior Notice is the FDA requirement most likely to catch first-time importers of Indian herbal ingredients by surprise - not because it is complicated, but because it is timing-critical in a way that most other import documentation is not. A Certificate of Analysis or Phytosanitary Certificate can be prepared days or weeks ahead with margin for error. Prior Notice has a narrow, mode-specific window that closes hours before arrival, and there is no mechanism to file it late.

This guide covers what Prior Notice requires, exactly when it must be filed by transport mode, who is responsible for filing it, and the specific failure patterns that hold herbal ingredient and dietary supplement shipments at US ports. If your supply chain is not yet FDA-compliant more broadly, start with our guide to [US FDA and DSHEA requirements for importing herbal ingredients from India](/blog/usa-fda-dshea-indian-herbal-ingredient-compliance/), which covers facility registration, cGMP, and NDI notifications - Prior Notice is a separate, per-shipment requirement that sits on top of those business-level compliance items.

## Direct Answer: What Prior Notice Is and Why It Applies

FDA Prior Notice is a mandatory advance notification required under the Public Health Security and Bioterrorism Preparedness and Response Act of 2002, codified at 21 CFR Part 1, Subpart I. It applies to all food, animal feed, and dietary supplement shipments imported or offered for import into the United States - which includes herbal and botanical ingredients, standardized extracts, and dietary supplement raw materials from India. Prior Notice must be submitted electronically and confirmed by FDA before the shipment physically arrives at the US port of entry. There is no exemption for herbal or botanical ingredients specifically; if the product is food or a dietary supplement ingredient under FDA's jurisdiction, Prior Notice applies.

---

## Why This Matters More Than It Looks

Prior Notice is easy to underweight in supply chain planning because, unlike FDA facility registration or an NDI notification, it is not a one-time compliance item - it is a per-shipment, per-product-article filing that must happen correctly every single time. A single ocean container carrying multiple distinct herbal ingredients requires a separate Prior Notice submission for each distinct article of food, not one blanket filing for the container. Buyers importing a mixed shipment - ashwagandha extract, moringa powder, and turmeric extract in the same container, for example - need to confirm their filer understands this and is not treating the shipment as a single Prior Notice event.

## Filing Windows: How Far Ahead and How Close to Arrival

Prior Notice has both a maximum advance window and a minimum lead-time requirement, and both must be satisfied.

**Maximum advance window (how early you can file):**

- **Through FDA's Prior Notice System Interface (PNSI):** no more than 15 calendar days before the anticipated date of arrival
- **Through a customs broker via CBP's Automated Broker Interface (ABI), part of the Automated Commercial Environment (ACE):** no more than 30 calendar days before the anticipated date of arrival

**Minimum lead time before arrival (how close to arrival is too late), by transport mode:**

- **Ocean freight:** at least 8 hours before arrival
- **Air freight, flight time 4 hours or more:** at the time of departure
- **Air freight, flight time under 4 hours:** before departure
- **Rail:** at least 4 hours before arrival
- **Road (from Canada or Mexico):** 2 to 4 hours before arrival at the border, depending on origin

For most India-to-US herbal ingredient shipments, which move by ocean freight, the practical rule is straightforward: Prior Notice must be confirmed by FDA at least 8 hours before the vessel's arrival, and can be filed up to 15 days ahead through PNSI or up to 30 days ahead if a customs broker is filing through ABI as part of standard entry processing. Most importers with an established customs broker will have Prior Notice filed automatically as part of the broker's ABI entry filing, coordinated with the separate Importer Security Filing (ISF) that ocean shipments also require - the two filings use overlapping but not identical data, so buyers should confirm their broker is filing both correctly rather than assuming one covers the other.

## Who Files, and What the Indian Exporter Needs to Provide

The US importer of record carries ultimate legal responsibility for Prior Notice being filed accurately and on time. The Indian exporter is not the filer of record, but the filing cannot happen correctly without information only the exporter can provide promptly.

**Filing is typically done by one of:**

- The importer directly, using their own FDA PNSI account
- The importer's customs broker, filing through ABI/ACE as part of the standard entry process - the most common path for established import relationships
- A dedicated FDA compliance service, particularly useful for buyers without an existing broker relationship set up for Prior Notice

**Information the Indian exporter needs to supply, ideally as a standing data set updated per shipment rather than requested fresh each time:**

- Current FDA facility registration number for the manufacturing facility (or the facility's full name and address if the registration number is genuinely unavailable)
- Accurate product description matching what is on the commercial invoice - vague or inconsistent product descriptions are a common cause of Prior Notice rejection
- Country of production
- Grower or source information, where known and applicable
- Anticipated shipment and arrival details once finalized - vessel name, expected port of arrival, and Bill of Lading or Airway Bill number

Buyers should treat this as a standing supplier-qualification item, not a one-off request: confirm the FDA facility registration number is current at the start of the relationship, and set an expectation that the exporter will flag promptly if that registration lapses or changes - facility registration must be renewed biennially, and a lapsed registration invalidates Prior Notice filings referencing it even if the rest of the filing is accurate.

## Common Errors That Cause Port Holds

**Missing or expired FDA facility registration number.** Prior Notice requires the manufacturer's registration number. If the Indian supplier's registration has lapsed - registration must be renewed biennially in even-numbered years - the Prior Notice references an invalid registration, and the shipment can be held even though every other document is in order.

**Inconsistent product descriptions across documents.** If the product description on the Prior Notice does not closely match the commercial invoice and packing list, FDA can flag the shipment for a discrepancy review. This is the same underlying discipline that prevents customs holds on the Indian export side - descriptions should match exactly across every document in the shipment file, not just approximately.

**Filing outside the permitted window.** Filing more than 15 days ahead through PNSI (or 30 days through ABI) results in rejection of the submission - it simply will not be accepted that early. Filing inside the maximum window but missing the minimum lead time before arrival means the shipment can physically arrive before Prior Notice is confirmed, which triggers the same refusal-of-admission consequence as not filing at all.

**Treating a multi-product container as a single filing.** As noted above, Prior Notice is required per distinct article of food. A container with three different herbal ingredients needs three Prior Notice submissions. Buyers consolidating shipments from multiple Indian suppliers or multiple product lines into a single container should confirm their broker is filing separately for each distinct product.

**Confusing Prior Notice with Importer Security Filing (ISF).** For ocean freight specifically, ISF (also called "10+2") is a separate CBP filing required 24 hours before the vessel departs the foreign port, with its own distinct data requirements. Prior Notice and ISF are both mandatory, both time-sensitive, and both require overlapping but not identical shipment data - treating them as the same filing, or assuming one broker submission covers both, is a common and avoidable error.

## Practical Coordination Checklist

For buyers establishing or scaling an India-to-US herbal ingredient supply relationship:

- Confirm the exporter's current FDA facility registration number before the first shipment, and re-confirm at each registration renewal cycle
- Establish with your customs broker exactly who is filing Prior Notice - the broker via ABI, or you directly via PNSI - so there is no ambiguity about responsibility
- Build shipment data handoff (vessel name, arrival port, Bill of Lading number) into your supplier's standard shipping notification process, timed to arrive with enough margin for your filer to meet the transport-mode-specific minimum lead time
- For multi-product containers, confirm your broker is filing Prior Notice separately for each distinct article, not treating the container as a single filing
- Keep a record of confirmed Prior Notice numbers per shipment for at least the FSVP-required retention period, since these records are subject to FDA inspection

## Summary

FDA Prior Notice is a mandatory, per-shipment, time-sensitive filing that applies to all herbal ingredient and dietary supplement imports from India, separate from and in addition to FDA facility registration and other business-level compliance requirements. It must be filed within a defined window - no more than 15 days ahead through PNSI or 30 days through ABI, and no later than a transport-mode-specific minimum before arrival, typically 8 hours for ocean freight. Responsibility sits with the US importer, but the filing depends on accurate, current information from the Indian exporter, particularly a valid FDA facility registration number. The most common failure modes are a lapsed registration, inconsistent product descriptions, missed timing windows, and treating a multi-product shipment as a single filing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is FDA Prior Notice and does it apply to herbal ingredient shipments from India?**

Prior Notice is a mandatory advance notification required under the 2002 Bioterrorism Act before any food or dietary supplement shipment arrives at a US port. It applies to herbal ingredients from India since they are food or dietary supplement articles under FDA jurisdiction. Shipments without a confirmed Prior Notice are subject to refusal of admission.

**How far in advance can Prior Notice be filed, and how close to arrival must it be filed?**

No more than 15 days ahead via PNSI or 30 days via a broker's ABI filing. On the near end, minimum lead time before arrival depends on transport mode - typically 8 hours for ocean freight, and shorter or departure-based windows for air, rail, and road.

**Who is responsible for filing Prior Notice - the Indian exporter or the US importer?**

The US importer of record is legally responsible. In practice, filing is done by the importer directly through PNSI, by their customs broker through ABI, or by a compliance service. The exporter's role is providing accurate, timely product and facility information to support the filing.

**What information does Prior Notice require that an Indian exporter needs to supply?**

Product description and quantity, the manufacturer's current FDA facility registration number, country of production, grower information where known, and shipment details including the Bill of Lading or Airway Bill number once finalized.

**What happens if Prior Notice is filed incorrectly or too late?**

The shipment is subject to refusal of admission and can be held at the port until an adequate Prior Notice is confirmed, risking spoilage for perishable material in addition to cost and delay. Failure to file is also a prohibited act under the FD&C Act, carrying separate civil and criminal exposure.
