Exporting Lakadong turmeric from India to the UAE requires a defined set of certificates and declarations - covering plant health, origin, quality, and customs classification - that must be assembled correctly before goods leave India and that UAE import rules require on arrival. Missing or mismatched documentation is one of the most common causes of customs delay for herbal ingredient shipments, and it is entirely avoidable with a documentation checklist agreed before the first order ships.

**Quick answer:** Exporting Lakadong turmeric to the UAE requires an Importer Exporter Code (IEC) and FSSAI license held by the Indian exporter, a commercial invoice and packing list, a Certificate of Origin, a phytosanitary certificate from India's Plant Quarantine authority, and a batch-specific Certificate of Analysis - classified under HS code 0910 30 and labelled on arrival to meet UAE country-of-origin and Arabic-language requirements.

## Why Does Documentation Matter for Lakadong Turmeric Specifically?

Lakadong turmeric's premium, GI-tagged positioning means buyers are often paying a price premium tied to origin and quality claims - which makes documentation that substantiates those claims (traceability to West Jaintia Hills, batch-specific curcumin testing) commercially important in addition to being a customs requirement. A shipment that clears UAE customs but cannot substantiate its origin or quality claims to the buyer's own downstream customers undermines the premium positioning the buyer is paying for.

## What HS Code Applies to Lakadong Turmeric?

Turmeric, including Lakadong turmeric, is classified under HS code 0910 30 in the internationally harmonised customs nomenclature. Both the Indian exporter's commercial invoice and the UAE import declaration should reference this code consistently, and buyers should confirm with their customs broker whether the UAE tariff schedule applies any additional digits beyond the six-digit harmonised code for more specific classification. For a broader walkthrough of how herbal ingredient HS classification works, see our [HS code classification guide](/blog/hs-code-classification-herbal-ingredients-india-export/).

## What Export Documents Does the Indian Supplier Need?

A complete Indian export documentation package for a Lakadong turmeric shipment includes:

1. **Importer Exporter Code (IEC)** - the exporting entity's registration to conduct international trade, issued by India's Directorate General of Foreign Trade.
2. **FSSAI license** - confirming the exporter's compliance with Indian food safety regulation.
3. **Commercial invoice and packing list** - itemising the shipment, quantities, and agreed terms.
4. **Certificate of Origin** - confirming Indian origin, issued by an authorised chamber of commerce.
5. **Phytosanitary certificate** - issued by India's Plant Quarantine authority, confirming plant health compliance for the agricultural consignment.
6. **Batch-specific Certificate of Analysis** - covering curcumin content (HPLC-tested), moisture, microbiological limits, heavy metals, and pesticide residues.

Buyers should request copies of all six documents in advance of shipment, not only on arrival, so discrepancies can be resolved before goods are in transit.

## Why Is the Phytosanitary Certificate Required?

Turmeric is an agricultural commodity, and most destination markets - including the UAE - require a phytosanitary certificate for agricultural consignments to confirm the shipment is free of regulated pests and meets plant health requirements. This certificate is issued by India's Plant Quarantine authority following inspection, and the inspection and issuance process should be factored into the shipment's overall lead time, particularly for a first-time supplier relationship where the process may not yet be routine.

## What Does the Certificate of Origin Confirm, and Why Does It Matter for CEPA?

The Certificate of Origin confirms that the Lakadong turmeric shipment genuinely originated in India, which matters both for standard customs clearance and for buyers seeking to claim preferential duty treatment under the India-UAE Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA). Buyers exploring whether their shipment qualifies for reduced duty under CEPA should review origin documentation requirements alongside our [India-UAE CEPA guide for herbal ingredients](/blog/india-uae-cepa-herbal-ingredients-gcc/), since CEPA preferential treatment depends on meeting specific rules-of-origin criteria, not simply on the goods being shipped from India.

## What Are UAE Food Import and Labelling Requirements?

UAE food import regulation requires that imported food products, including turmeric, meet applicable UAE food safety standards and be accompanied by required certification on arrival. Separately, UAE labelling rules require imported food product labels to display clear country-of-origin information along with Arabic-language explanatory information - covering product name, ingredients, net weight, and any required warnings. Buyers should agree with their supplier at what stage in the supply chain Arabic labelling will be applied: at origin in India, at a UAE bonded warehouse, or by the importer after customs clearance.

## Does Lakadong Turmeric Require Halal Certification for GCC Distribution?

Halal certification is not universally mandatory for raw turmeric imports into the UAE, but it becomes commercially relevant where the turmeric will be incorporated into food, beverage, or nutraceutical products marketed as Halal, or where downstream GCC distribution partners require it as a condition of stocking the product. Buyers planning onward distribution into Saudi Arabia or other GCC markets with stricter Halal enforcement should review requirements in our [Halal certification guide for UAE and GCC markets](/blog/halal-certification-indian-herbal-ingredients-uae-gcc/) before finalising a supplier agreement.

## What Testing Documentation Should Accompany the Shipment?

Beyond the phytosanitary and origin certificates, buyers should require a batch-specific Certificate of Analysis referencing the same lot number printed on the shipment's packaging - covering HPLC-tested curcumin content, moisture, microbiological limits (total plate count, yeast and mould, E. coli, Salmonella), heavy metals (lead, arsenic, cadmium, mercury), and pesticide residues tested against the applicable regulatory standard. For a wider view of the certificate types commonly required across herbal ingredient imports, see our [CoA, MSDS, and phytosanitary certificate guide](/blog/coa-msds-phytosanitary-certificates-herbal-imports/).

## Who Handles Documentation - Buyer or Supplier?

Export-side documentation (IEC, FSSAI, Certificate of Origin, phytosanitary certificate, export-side Certificate of Analysis) is the exporting Indian supplier's responsibility. Import-side compliance - customs declaration in the UAE, any import permits, and confirming the product and its labelling meet UAE food safety and labelling rules - is the UAE-based buyer or importer's responsibility. A clear written understanding of this division of responsibility before the first shipment avoids delays caused by each party assuming the other is handling a given document.

## A Pre-Shipment Documentation Checklist

Before confirming shipment of a Lakadong turmeric order, buyers should verify: IEC and FSSAI license on file for the supplier; commercial invoice and packing list matching the agreed order; Certificate of Origin issued and referencing the correct HS code; phytosanitary certificate issued for the specific consignment; batch-specific Certificate of Analysis referencing the same lot number as the physical goods; and a documented plan for Arabic-language labelling before the product reaches UAE retail or food-service channels.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What HS code applies to Lakadong turmeric exports?**

Lakadong turmeric, like other Indian turmeric, is classified under HS code 0910 30 in international trade documentation. Buyers and suppliers should confirm the full national tariff-line extension used at the UAE port of entry, since HS classification can extend to additional digits at the country level even where the first six digits are internationally harmonised.

**Is a phytosanitary certificate required for Lakadong turmeric?**

Yes. Agricultural consignments including turmeric require a phytosanitary certificate issued by India's Plant Quarantine authority confirming the shipment meets plant health requirements for export. This certificate is issued per shipment and should be requested well in advance of the planned shipment date, since quarantine inspection scheduling can add lead time.

**Does UAE food law require Arabic labelling for imported turmeric?**

Yes. UAE food import rules require imported food products to carry clear country-of-origin information along with Arabic-language explanatory information on the product label. This labelling requirement applies regardless of whether the primary label is applied at origin in India or after import in the UAE, and buyers should confirm at what stage in the supply chain Arabic labelling will be added.

**What is a Certificate of Origin and why is it required?**

A Certificate of Origin is an official document confirming that a shipment originated in India, typically issued by an authorised Indian chamber of commerce or export promotion body. It supports customs clearance in the UAE and is also the document that underpins preferential duty treatment where applicable, such as under the India-UAE Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA).

**Who is responsible for export documentation - the buyer or the supplier?**

The exporting supplier is generally responsible for preparing and issuing export-side documentation - the commercial invoice, packing list, Certificate of Origin, phytosanitary certificate, and Certificate of Analysis. The UAE-based buyer or importer is generally responsible for import-side compliance, including customs declaration, any required import permits, and ensuring the product and its labelling meet UAE food safety and labelling regulations on arrival.
